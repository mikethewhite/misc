Prepare a source-linked summary of my documented work interactions with
[Full Name], employee ID [ID], during [start date] through [end date].

Search the chat messages, emails, meeting notes or transcripts, and work
documents available within my authorized access. Include relevant referenced
materials where they explicitly document this person’s involvement. Use the
employee ID to disambiguate identity, not as an additional research target.

Your task is to retrieve, extract, and organize existing information so I can
interpret the underlying records myself. Focus on what was documented:
actions, deliverables, decisions, outcomes, statements, and follow-ups.
Do not infer personal traits, motivations, competence, or suitability.
Do not assign ratings, rank the individual, or recommend actions about them.

Organize the information under these four themes:

1. Delivery and business outcomes
Identify projects, deliverables, operational responsibilities, problems
addressed, decisions made, and assistance provided. Describe the individual’s
documented contribution and any recorded effects on the team, business,
Firm, or clients.

Include measurable results where available. Distinguish achieved outcomes
from intended benefits, estimates, and self-reported claims. Where a result
belongs to a team, do not attribute it to the individual unless the source
establishes their specific contribution.

2. Risk, controls, and process-related activities
Identify documented involvement in control execution, approvals, adherence
to procedures, risk discussions, escalation, incident handling, remediation,
audit work, and policy or standards-related decisions.

Capture recorded explanations of risk or control decisions, concerns raised,
actions taken, and resolution status. Describe the record without independently
concluding that the person complied with or violated a policy, regulation,
or law. Preserve the distinction between an allegation, an acknowledged issue,
and an established finding.

3. Collaboration, knowledge sharing, and participation
Identify concrete examples of cross-team coordination, knowledge sharing,
assistance to colleagues, stakeholder communication, seeking differing
perspectives, acknowledging others’ contributions, and enabling participation.

Include explicit references to the Firm’s stated values where present in
the source material. Describe the actions and context rather than converting
them into personality labels or judgments about “culture fit.” Do not infer
anyone’s demographic characteristics or personal circumstances.

4. Team direction and employee support, where applicable
Where the individual’s people-management responsibilities are established,
identify documented goal-setting, delegation, expectation-setting, coaching,
development discussions, workload planning, follow-through, and steps to
support participation and access to opportunities.

Describe specific activities and recorded outcomes. Do not infer management
effectiveness from communication style, popularity, or frequency of contact.
If this theme is not applicable or supported by the records, say so.

Create a separate section for feedback already present in the records.
Retrieve explicit recognition, concerns, requests for changes, suggestions
for improvement, or development advice that was actually communicated.
For each item, identify:
- Who provided it, when, and in what context.
- What was said, using a short quotation or faithful paraphrase.
- Any specific action requested or agreed.
- Any documented response, follow-up, or resolution.
- The supporting source.

Treat feedback as an attributed statement, not as your own conclusion.
Do not invent additional strengths, weaknesses, or development suggestions.

Evidence requirements:
Support each substantive entry with a source reference, date, and direct
link or available identifier. Distinguish documented actions and outcomes
from attributed opinions and unresolved claims.

Include relevant positive, critical, and mixed evidence without forcing a
particular balance. Deduplicate repeated references to the same event.
Represent the full time period rather than concentrating only on recent
or frequently discussed work.

Do not treat message volume, response speed, tone, after-hours activity,
meeting attendance, or missing records as evidence of contribution quality.
Do not infer meeting content from a calendar entry alone.

Exclude unrelated personal information and sensitive personal details.
Where records conflict or attribution is uncertain, state the limitation.
Where a theme has little evidence, say “Limited evidence in the sources
reviewed,” rather than drawing a negative conclusion.

Output:
A. A brief scope note describing the period and sources actually reviewed,
   including important access or coverage limitations.
B. An evidence table grouped by the four themes, with columns for:
   date/period; project or topic; documented action or contribution;
   recorded outcome or relevance; source; and uncertainty or missing context.
C. The separate table of explicitly recorded feedback and follow-ups.
D. Recurring documented activities or topics, supported by multiple examples,
   without turning those patterns into judgments about the person.
E. Outstanding factual questions or missing records for me to verify.

Prioritize substantive examples over routine correspondence. Do not force
content into every theme. If a requested statement would require an unsupported
judgment, omit that judgment and provide the relevant source evidence instead.
